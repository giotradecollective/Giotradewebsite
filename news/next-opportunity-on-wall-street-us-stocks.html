The Next Opportunity on Wall Street: Where Should Investors Look in US Stocks?

By Ethan R. Hayes, Markets & Investment Analyst  ·  Markets & Investment

Artificial intelligence continues to reshape the American stock market, but with high expectations and uneven performance, investors may need to look beyond the biggest technology companies to find the next opportunities.

Wall Street's Rally Is Hiding a Different Story

The US stock market remains one of the world's most important investment destinations. Its largest companies influence global capital flows, technological development and investor sentiment. Yet beneath the headline gains, the market tells a more complicated story.

According to Reuters reporting on 12 October 2026, the S&P 500 had risen approximately 14% during the year, while more than 40% of its constituent stocks remained down year to date. The figures highlight how a relatively small group of leading companies can lift an index while many other shares struggle.

For investors, this creates an important question: will the next phase of market growth remain concentrated in artificial intelligence and semiconductor companies, or will opportunities emerge elsewhere?

The answer matters because buying the market's biggest winners after a strong rally is not necessarily the same as identifying its next investment opportunity.

Artificial Intelligence: The Opportunity Beyond the Hype

Artificial intelligence remains one of the most significant investment themes in global markets. Technology companies are spending heavily on advanced processors, data centres, networking equipment and the infrastructure required to power increasingly complex computing systems.

However, the opportunity extends beyond the companies developing AI software. It also includes the businesses supplying the equipment and infrastructure behind the technology.

NVIDIA remains central to AI computing through its specialised processors and computing platforms. Its future performance will depend on whether demand continues to support its earnings growth and whether its valuation leaves room for further returns.

Broadcom offers exposure to custom semiconductors and networking technology used in large computing systems. Its prospects are linked to continued investment by major technology companies and their ability to turn AI infrastructure spending into profitable services.

ASML occupies a different position in the supply chain. Its advanced lithography equipment is essential to manufacturing leading edge semiconductors. Reuters reported on 14 October 2026 that the company had raised its full year sales forecast amid strong AI related demand.

These companies offer different ways to participate in the AI investment cycle. But investors must distinguish between a promising industry and an attractively priced share.

Even if AI adoption continues to accelerate, shareholders can lose money if earnings fail to meet expectations already reflected in the share price. The next opportunity may therefore lie not simply in identifying who benefits from AI, but in determining which businesses can sustain profitable growth without requiring unrealistic assumptions.

Beyond Technology: Three Areas Worth Watching

Healthcare and Medical Technology

Healthcare offers exposure to medical innovation, ageing populations and demand for treatments, devices and healthcare services.

Companies such as Stryker and Johnson & Johnson provide exposure to different parts of the industry. Their investment prospects depend on product development, demand, competitive positioning and the ability to generate consistent earnings.

Healthcare is not automatically a defensive investment. Regulatory changes, litigation, rising costs and disappointing product results can all pressure share prices. Nevertheless, the sector offers a different set of growth drivers from the technology companies dominating current market attention.

Financial Services

Banks and asset managers offer another way to approach the US market, particularly as investors reassess interest rates, lending conditions and capital market activity.

JPMorgan Chase provides exposure to diversified banking and financial services, while Citigroup offers a potential restructuring story for investors examining whether operational improvements can translate into stronger profitability. BlackRock, meanwhile, benefits from its position in asset management, although its performance is influenced by market levels and investor flows.

The important distinction is between a company that appears cheap and one that is genuinely improving. Lower valuations can create opportunities, but they can also reflect deteriorating earnings, credit risks or persistent operational problems.

Energy and Power Infrastructure

The expansion of AI data centres raises another investment question: how will electricity supply keep pace with growing computing demand?

The potential beneficiaries extend beyond technology companies to electricity generation and supporting infrastructure.

Constellation Energy is one company worth researching because of its electricity generation business and exposure to power demand. Its prospects depend on electricity prices, operating performance, contractual arrangements and regulation.

The broader theme is that technological growth requires physical infrastructure. However, rising electricity demand does not automatically guarantee higher profits for every energy company. Capital costs, construction delays and market conditions will determine which businesses ultimately benefit.

The Real Question: What Are Investors Paying?

A successful company is not always a successful investment.

Consider a business whose earnings are growing rapidly but whose share price already assumes several years of exceptional growth. Even if the company continues performing well, its stock could fall if investors decide that future growth will be slower than expected.

A slower growing company may offer a better investment opportunity if its valuation is more reasonable and its earnings are more predictable.

This is why investors should look beyond headlines and share price momentum. Valuation, profitability, debt and cash generation all influence whether a company's future prospects justify its current market price.

The same principle applies to the wider market. If investors have crowded into a handful of popular stocks, even a modest disappointment could trigger a reassessment of their valuations. Meanwhile, companies receiving less attention may become more attractive if their financial performance improves while expectations remain relatively low.

This does not mean investors should automatically avoid expensive stocks or buy overlooked ones. It means that price and expectations matter just as much as the underlying business.

What Could Go Wrong?

Several risks could undermine the current US investment outlook.

First, AI spending may fail to generate the profits investors expect. Companies can invest billions in new infrastructure, but the ultimate returns depend on whether customers are willing to pay enough for the resulting services.

Second, persistent inflation could keep interest rates elevated, increasing borrowing costs and placing pressure on company valuations. Conversely, rate cuts prompted by a sharp economic slowdown might not help equities if corporate earnings deteriorate.

Third, geopolitical tensions, trade restrictions and semiconductor export controls could disrupt supply chains and increase costs for US companies operating internationally.

Finally, market concentration remains a concern. Investors who own several large technology shares may believe they are diversified, yet their investments could still depend on the same AI spending cycle and investor sentiment.

The key is not to predict every market setback. It is to recognise that attractive long term themes can still experience substantial short term losses.

What This Means for South African Investors

For South Africans, investing in US shares introduces an additional consideration: the rand.

A US share can rise in dollar terms while a stronger rand reduces the investment's return when converted back into local currency. A weaker rand can have the opposite effect.

This means offshore investment performance depends on both the underlying share price and exchange rate movements.

US equities can provide access to international businesses, different industries and sources of growth that may not be readily available on the Johannesburg Stock Exchange. However, investors must also account for brokerage fees, currency conversion costs, applicable taxes and the risks associated with foreign markets.

Offshore investing should therefore be considered within the context of an investor's overall portfolio, financial objectives and tolerance for risk, rather than as an automatic solution to South Africa's economic challenges.

GIOTRADE Network® Insight

The next opportunity on Wall Street may not come from buying the companies that have already attracted the most attention. It may emerge from identifying where earnings growth is sustainable, where expectations have become excessive and where businesses are improving without receiving the same level of market enthusiasm.

Artificial intelligence remains a major investment theme, but healthcare, financial services and power infrastructure offer other avenues for research. Each carries distinct risks, and none guarantees attractive returns.

For South African investors, the decision also involves currency exposure and the costs of investing offshore.

Ultimately, the question is not simply which US share could rise next. It is which business can deliver enough future value to justify its current price.

The strongest investment opportunities are not always found in the most popular companies, but in the gap between what the market expects and what a business can realistically deliver.
